Gaussian kernel-mixture volumes represent a participating medium as a sum of anisotropic Gaussian
primitives, each contributing an analytic, invertible optical depth. The representation, introduced by
\emph{Don't Splat Your Gaussians}, admits physically based path tracing, but the reference renderer
that established its correctness---built in Mitsuba---reaches a scattering event by marching
segment-by-segment between primitive boundaries, maintaining the set of overlapping primitives and
root-finding the scatter distance within each segment. This thesis asks whether a renderer built around the one
property the representation affords---the analytic per-primitive optical depth---can be both correct
and substantially faster.

It presents a from-scratch CUDA/OptiX volumetric path tracer organised around a novel rendering
architecture with two complementary halves. \emph{Single-trace any-hit collection} gathers every
primitive a ray enters in a single acceleration-structure traversal, using a back-face-culled
any-hit program and computing exits in closed form rather than re-tracing. \emph{Analog-decomposition
scatter sampling} then lets each collected primitive draw an independent closed-form free flight, and
takes the nearest---the argmin---as the scattering event. Together they replace the reference's
segment-marching, boundary-sorting, and root-finding outright.

Correctness is established by a differential-validation methodology that compares the renderer against
Mitsuba and, where a closed form exists, against analytic ground truth; the same ladder serves as the
unbiasedness evidence for the new sampler. A systematic performance-engineering study, measured across
time, image quality, and memory, contributes a second algorithmic win---volumetric product-resampled
direct lighting---alongside a rigorously profiled ledger of negative results that maps where effort on
this class of renderer does and does not pay off. Together these close an initial roughly fivefold
equal-quality deficit against the reference and overtake it on the showcase scene, free of its
fireflies.

\end{abstract}
