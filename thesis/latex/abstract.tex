Gaussian kernel-mixture volumes represent a participating medium as a sum of anisotropic Gaussian
primitives, each contributing an analytic, invertible optical depth. The representation, introduced by
\emph{Don't Splat Your Gaussians}, admits physically based path tracing, but the reference renderer
that established the representation's correctness---built in Mitsuba---reaches a scattering event by marching
segment-by-segment between primitive boundaries, maintaining the set of overlapping primitives and
root-finding the scatter distance within each segment. This thesis asks whether a renderer built around the one
property the representation affords---the analytic per-primitive optical depth---can be both correct
and substantially faster.

It presents a from-scratch CUDA/OptiX volumetric path tracer organised around a rendering
architecture with two complementary halves. \emph{Single-trace any-hit collection} gathers every
primitive a ray enters in a single acceleration-structure traversal, using a back-face-culled
any-hit program and computing exits in closed form rather than re-tracing. \emph{Analog-decomposition
scatter sampling}---a direction the reference's authors anticipated but did not implement---then lets
each collected primitive draw an independent closed-form free flight, and takes the nearest---the
argmin---as the scattering event. Together they replace the reference's segment-marching and
root-finding outright.

Correctness is established by a differential-validation methodology that compares the renderer against
Mitsuba and, where a closed form exists, against analytic ground truth, and is cross-checked against an
independent dense voxel-grid integrator; the same ladder serves as the unbiasedness evidence for the new
sampler. On the environment-lit showcase the renderer is both correct and fast: its
multiple-importance-sampled direct lighting matches the unbiased ground truth to within Monte-Carlo
noise, whereas the reference's faster estimator is energy-biased on this medium---so measured against the
reference's only unbiased (analog) configuration, it reaches a given noise level roughly $59\times$
faster at equal quality, free of that configuration's fireflies, and on less device memory ($578$
against $838$\,MiB on the same scene). A systematic performance-engineering study, measured across time
and image quality, contributes a second, scene-dependent win---volumetric product-resampled direct
lighting, $1.4\times$ at equal quality under environment lighting---alongside a profiled ledger of
negative results that maps where effort on this class of renderer does and does not pay off.

\end{abstract}
